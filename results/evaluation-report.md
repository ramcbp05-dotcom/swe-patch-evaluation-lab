## How close the near-misses get

Rank the incorrect candidates by how much of the issue suite they pass. The top
of this list is where the lab earns its keep: these are the patches that would
survive a code review.

| `variance-cancellation` / `wrong_clamp_negative` | 15 / 21 | 71 % | every shift-invariance test |
| `shape-mismatch-pairwise-distance` / `wrong_squared_distance` | 10 / 14 | 71 % | the two tests that distinguish a distance from its square |
| `silent-unsorted-interpolation` / `wrong_allows_duplicates` | 10 / 13 | 77 % | the three duplicate-abscissa tests |

Six of the twenty incorrect patches pass at least two thirds of their issue
suite: five strictly above it, and `wrong_rounded_weights` exactly at 16/24.
That is the number worth quoting: the gap between "fixes the reported symptom"
and "fixes the defect" is usually a handful of tests, and they are not the
obvious ones.

| `variance-cancellation` / `wrong_ignores_ddof` | 3 |

`wrong_ignores_ddof` is the one to look at. Its fail-to-pass failures are about
the estimator, but it *also* regresses three pass-to-pass tests, because
changing `variance` from sample to population silently moves every existing
caller's answer. That is what the pass-to-pass set is for: a patch can satisfy
the issue and still be unmergeable.

## Reproducing

```bash
./scripts/run_evaluation.sh
```

```bash
python -m evaluation.cli evaluate -c variance-cancellation -p wrong_ignores_ddof
```

The harness builds a fresh sandbox per run, applies `bug.patch`, confirms the
issue tests fail, applies the candidate, and runs both suites. Nothing is
cached between runs, so these numbers reproduce exactly.
